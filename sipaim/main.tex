\begin{abstract}
Semi-supervised learning methods for medical image segmentation, or SSL methods, stop using the unlabeled data once training converges, even though the model's predictions on it still carry residual uncertainty concentrated at organ boundaries. This paper shows that this residual entropy is spatially structured and exploits it with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}, or PEM: any converged SSL checkpoint is fine-tuned for two to five epochs with a single entropy-minimization loss on unlabeled data, using no labels, no pseudo-labels, and no architectural changes. A label-free diagnostic $\rho_f$ predicts in advance when PEM will help, and a gradient analysis explains why the loss localizes to the boundary with no explicit mask, and why masking nonetheless helps, through the loss normalization rather than the gating. Applied to the publicly released Bidirectional Copy-Paste, or BCP, checkpoints with no test-set tuning, PEM reduces the 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance by $27$--$43\%$, consistent with a boundary-refinement mechanism, and improves the Dice score by $+1.12$, $+1.27$, and $+0.62$ points on Pancreas-CT at 20\% labeled, Left Atrium at 5\% labeled, and Left Atrium at 10\% labeled, in under five minutes per run on a single GPU. Against temperature scaling, pseudo-label fine-tuning, and self-consistency under an identical protocol, PEM obtains the largest mean gain on the two less-saturated configurations, every gain significant at $p<0.025$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Manual voxel-level annotation of 3D medical scans is slow and expensive, which makes semi-supervised learning, or SSL, training from a small labeled set together with a large unlabeled set, a practical necessity for volumetric segmentation. Recent SSL methods for this task~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher,yu2019uamt,bai2023bcp,liu2024most,assefa2025dycon} reach strong accuracy by using the unlabeled data during training and then discarding it at convergence.

This paper makes a complementary observation: at convergence, a well-trained SSL model still assigns non-trivial prediction entropy to the unlabeled volumes, concentrated in a thin shell of low-confidence voxels on the predicted organ boundary. This concentration is a property of SSL convergence, not of any single method, and it is exactly the condition under which a label-free sharpening step is well-behaved, since the gradient it produces is already aligned with the locus of segmentation error.

This is exploited with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}, or PEM: any converged SSL checkpoint is fine-tuned for a few epochs on the unlabeled data with a single entropy objective, using no labels, pseudo-labels, thresholds, or architectural changes, in minutes on one GPU, on top of a released checkpoint without retraining. Entropy minimization itself is classical~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} and standard in modern SSL~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch}; what is new is its operating regime and the three results that follow.

\noindent\textbf{A label-free diagnostic.}
The first contribution is the \textit{shell entropy concentration} $\rho_f$, a scalar that turns the observation above into a test. For a converged checkpoint, $\rho_f$ is the fraction of total prediction entropy that the low-confidence voxels carry on the unlabeled set; it needs neither labels nor a validation set and follows from a single forward pass. It separates checkpoints on which post-hoc sharpening is well-conditioned from those on which it is not: the three released BCP checkpoints hold $89$ to $95\%$ of their prediction entropy in a thin boundary shell of a few percent of the voxels, whereas a supervised-only network on the same labels keeps only $55\%$ there and scatters the rest through the interior, as Table~\ref{tab:entropy} reports.

\noindent\textbf{PEM and why it works.}
The second contribution is PEM together with an account of why the classical loss needs no stabilizers in this regime. The entropy-minimization gradient is supported on a band of moderate confidence flanking the boundary and vanishes both on the saturated interior and at the point of maximum ambiguity, so it gates itself to the boundary shell with no explicit mask; the confidence mask instead acts through the loss normalization. Because partial-volume averaging and weak soft-tissue contrast make the anatomical-boundary voxels the ambiguous ones, the feature-space decision surface co-locates with the physical organ surface, and entropy minimization there realizes the cluster assumption as boundary refinement. The same argument predicts the two regimes in which PEM should fail, and the entropy maps in Fig.~\ref{fig:qual} show the boundary shell it acts on.

\noindent\textbf{Validation across organs and modalities.}
The third contribution is an empirical study spanning two organs and two imaging modalities. On the released BCP checkpoints for Pancreas-CT and Left Atrium, with no test-set tuning and a budget fixed in advance, PEM improves both overlap and boundary metrics and outperforms three controlled post-hoc baselines, namely temperature scaling, pseudo-label fine-tuning, and self-consistency, under an identical protocol, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:main}. Every gain is significant under a per-case Wilcoxon signed-rank test, the ablation over the mask and learning rate in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates the confidence-shell design, and PEM degrades a checkpoint only when its $\rho_f$ falls below the suitability threshold, exactly as the diagnostic predicts.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\noindent\textbf{SSL for medical image segmentation.}
Two mechanisms dominate: \emph{consistency regularization}, where a student network is trained to agree with a teacher or with itself under perturbations, and \emph{pseudo-labeling}, where confident predictions become hard targets. The Mean Teacher framework~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher} uses an exponential-moving-average (EMA) teacher; Uncertainty-Aware Mean Teacher (UA-MT)~\cite{yu2019uamt} filters the target by uncertainty; DTC~\cite{luo2022dtc} and MC-Net+~\cite{wu2022mcnet} enforce consistency across heads or decoders; Cross Pseudo Supervision (CPS)~\cite{chen2021cps} combines both. Bidirectional Copy-Paste (BCP)~\cite{bai2023bcp} mixes labeled and unlabeled crops bidirectionally and is a strong, widely used baseline; MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} improve further but release training code only. All share a training-time contract: the unlabeled data is used during optimization and never revisited. PEM revisits what it still contains at convergence; the novelty is not entropy minimization but characterizing the SSL-convergence regime, with a label-free diagnostic, in which it becomes well-conditioned.

\noindent\textbf{Entropy minimization.}
Entropy minimization~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} adds $-\lambda H(Y\mid X)$ on unlabeled data and implements the low-density-separation principle of the cluster assumption~\cite{chapelle2006ssl}; Pseudo-Label~\cite{lee2013pseudolabel} is its hard-target limit, and modern methods such as FixMatch~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch} sharpen predictions to form pseudo-targets. In all of these the entropy term is active \emph{during} training, coupled to a supervised or consistency loss that prevents posterior collapse, and requires careful balancing over time. PEM uses the same formal loss but in a different regime: applied to an already-converged checkpoint, the residual entropy it sees is concentrated at boundaries, so the gradient is self-localized and none of the usual stabilizing machinery is needed.

\noindent\textbf{Test-time adaptation.}
The closest methodology is test-time adaptation. TENT~\cite{wang2021tent} minimizes prediction entropy on a test stream by updating only normalization parameters; SHOT~\cite{liang2020shot} adapts a frozen classifier by entropy minimization with information maximization; and EATA~\cite{niu2022eata} and SAR~\cite{niu2023sar} add entropy-based sample selection, the same masking question as Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, and document the collapse unrestricted entropy minimization can cause. All share PEM's loss but assume a distribution shift, adapting a parameter subset against a moving target stream. PEM makes no shift assumption, operates on training-distribution data, updates all parameters, and yields a single frozen-weights checkpoint; in the SSL-convergence regime the collapse they guard against is bounded away (Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}).

\noindent\textbf{Post-hoc calibration and refinement.}
Temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration} rescales logits by a scalar and, being monotone, cannot change the argmax and hence cannot move any overlap metric; it is included to rule out recalibration. Stochastic weight averaging~\cite{izmailov2018swa} and self-distillation~\cite{hinton2015distillation} refine a model without new labels but need the training trajectory or hard-target distillation, closer to the pseudo-label baseline.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Shell Entropy Concentration}
Let $f_{\theta^*}$ be a converged checkpoint and $\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}$ an unlabeled volume. For voxel $i$ with per-class softmax probabilities $p_{i,c}$, define the per-voxel entropy $H_i=-\sum_c p_{i,c}\log p_{i,c}$ and confidence $c_i=\max_c p_{i,c}$. The \textit{shell entropy concentration} at confidence threshold $\tau$ is
\begin{equation}
\rho_f(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\,\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau]\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\right]} \in [0,1].
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
$\rho_f(\tau)$ is the fraction of total prediction entropy carried by voxels of confidence at most $\tau$. It is label-free, scalar, and self-localizing: on a converged network the low-confidence voxels lie near the decision boundary. Throughout, $\tau=0.99$, and $\rho^\star=0.85$ is a provisional suitability threshold, placed below the SSL checkpoints and well above the supervised baseline of Table~\ref{tab:entropy} and left to be pinned down by the denser checkpoint sweep discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\subsection{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}
PEM fine-tunes the checkpoint on the unlabeled set with the entropy loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{PEM}} \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}}\frac{\sum_{i} m_i H_i}{\sum_i m_i+\epsilon},
\qquad m_i=\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau],
\label{eq:pem}
\end{equation}
optionally restricted to the confidence shell through the mask $m_i$. No labels or pseudo-labels are used. Fine-tuning runs for a small, fixed number of epochs $E$ with a small learning rate.

\section{Analysis: Why PEM Works}
\label{sec:analysis}
\textit{The gradient.} The analysis is stated for a binary voxel with logit $z$ and probability $p=\sigma(z)$; the multi-class case replaces $z\,p(1-p)$ with the analogous per-logit softmax term. The per-voxel entropy gradient is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial H(p(z))}{\partial z} \;=\; \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\cdot\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} \;=\; (-z)\cdot p(1-p) \;=\; -\,z\,p(1-p).
\label{eq:dHdz}
\end{equation}
This product vanishes at three points, $p\to0$, $p\to1$, and $p=0.5$ where $z=0$, and is maximized on the moderately confident voxels at $|z|\approx1.54$, i.e.\ $p\approx0.82$ or $0.18$. The entropy gradient is therefore supported on a band of moderate confidence flanking the boundary: negligible on the saturated interior and at maximum ambiguity, largest on the partially decided voxels beside it.

\begin{proposition}[Boundary-band gradient support]
\label{prop:gate}
For the unmasked entropy loss, the magnitude of each voxel's gradient is proportional to $|z_i|\,p_i(1{-}p_i)$, which vanishes at $p_i\to0$, at $p_i\to1$, and at $p_i=0.5$, and is maximized at $|z_i|\approx1.54$. The gradient is thus automatically supported on the moderate-confidence band flanking the decision surface, with no explicit confidence mask.
\end{proposition}

\textit{Gating versus normalization.} Proposition~\ref{prop:gate} fixes which voxels receive a gradient but not the step size, and the two reconcile Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. The gating is automatic and the mask leaves it unchanged; the mask changes the normalization. Eq.~\ref{eq:pem} is a masked mean over $\sum_i m_i$; unmasked it averages over the whole volume, $91$ to $95\%$ of which is saturated, diluting the shell by $15$ to $20\times$. Masking removes the dilution and scales the effective shell step, which is why the confident-shell loss beats the unmasked loss at every learning rate although both gate the same voxels; a looser threshold readmits the interior and recovers the weaker behavior.

\textit{Two bounded properties.} \emph{(i)} At small learning rate and high $\rho_f$, sharpening does not collapse a converged checkpoint, since the gradient vanishes on committed voxels; the bound weakens as $\rho_f$ falls or the learning rate grows, where PEM degrades the checkpoint, as on the supervised baseline and above $5\times10^{-5}$. \emph{(ii)} The mean gradient scales with $\bar V=\mathbb{E}_i[p_i(1{-}p_i)]$, so as $\rho_f\to1$ and $\bar V\to0$ the gain diminishes and pseudo-label fine-tuning, whose gradient does not vanish on committed voxels, becomes competitive.

\textit{Refinement, not relocation.} The step has the sign of $z_i$, committing a boundary voxel to its more probable class, so it refines a boundary that SSL convergence placed correctly and reinforces the error otherwise. Because partial-volume averaging and weak soft-tissue contrast make the anatomical-boundary voxels the ambiguous ones, the feature-space decision surface co-locates with the physical organ surface, so entropy minimization realizes the cluster assumption~\cite{chapelle2006ssl,grandvalet2004entropy} as boundary refinement; $\rho_f$ measures whether the uncertain voxels form that shell or scatter through the interior.

\section{Datasets and Implementation}
\label{sec:data}
\noindent\textbf{Datasets.}
Evaluation uses two public benchmarks. Pancreas-CT~\cite{roth2015pancreas} contains abdominal Computed Tomography (CT) scans; following the standard split, 12 labeled, 50 unlabeled, and 18 test cases are used (20\% labeled). The Left Atrium (LA) 2018 dataset contains cardiac Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) scans; the standard 5\% (4 labeled) and 10\% (8 labeled) splits with 20 test cases are used. Both use the preprocessing distributed with BCP~\cite{bai2023bcp}.

\noindent\textbf{Checkpoints and protocol.}
The experiments start from the publicly released BCP checkpoints (V-Net backbone; instance normalization for Pancreas, batch normalization for LA). PEM uses Adam (no weight decay or schedule), gradient clipping at $1$, and learning rates $5\times10^{-5}$ (Pancreas) and $1\times10^{-5}$ (LA) chosen by 5-fold cross-validation on labeled folds; LA batch-normalization statistics are frozen. The main results use the confident-shell loss with $\tau=0.99$ throughout. The budget is fixed \textit{a priori} at $E=2$ (Pancreas) and $E=5$ (LA), and PEM numbers are the mean over seeds $\{2020,42,123\}$. Inference uses sliding windows with BCP's strides and no test-time augmentation (TTA); each run finishes in under $5.2$ minutes on one RTX 4070 Ti SUPER.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics and post-hoc baselines.}
The metrics are Dice and Jaccard (higher is better) and the 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance (HD95) and Average Surface Distance (ASD) in voxels (lower is better). Three post-hoc baselines receive the same checkpoint, budget, learning rate, and data:
\textit{Temperature Scaling} (TS) fits a scalar temperature on unlabeled entropy and cannot change the argmax;
\textit{Pseudo-Label Fine-Tuning} (PL-FT) generates hard pseudo-labels (argmax then largest connected component, CC) and fine-tunes with cross-entropy plus Dice;
\textit{Self-Consistency} (SC) minimizes the mean-squared error between the student on a noised input and the frozen teacher on the clean input.
The comparison is against BCP because MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} release training code only.

\noindent\textbf{Mask and threshold ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).}
Restricting the loss to the confidence shell helps through normalization, not gating (Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}): the masked loss beats the unmasked one at every learning rate, peaking near $\tau=0.95$, with the gain growing $30$--$60\%$ from LR $5\times10^{-6}$ to $10^{-5}$. Since $m_i=\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau]$, a tight $\tau=0.90$ isolates the boundary band while a loose $\tau=0.999$ readmits the interior and approaches the unmasked loss. The learning rate has a hard floor and ceiling: below $10^{-6}$ no update occurs, above $5\times10^{-5}$ the boundary is relocated. The main results fix $\tau=0.99$ \textit{a priori}, with Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reporting the sensitivity to $\tau$. Two choices are confirmed on Pancreas-CT: a disagreement mask~\cite{yu2019uamt} is empty on a converged checkpoint and yields no update, so the confidence shell is the right post-hoc mask; and updating only the decoder or EMA weights gives $\le0$ gain, so PEM updates all student parameters, unlike normalization-only TTA~\cite{wang2021tent}. Dice is monotonic in $E$ to a peak at $E=8$; the conservative \textit{a priori} $E=5$ is reported.

\noindent\textbf{Convergence is immediate.}
Most of the gain arrives in the first epoch: HD95 drops sharply at epoch~$1$ (e.g.\ $13.9\!\to\!8.4$ voxels on LA 5\%) while Dice rises monotonically and plateaus within the budget, confirming that $E\!\in\!\{2,5\}$ suffices and the gain is a stable refinement, not a lucky early stop.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This work shows that converged SSL checkpoints leave a structured, boundary-localized residual entropy, quantifies it with the label-free diagnostic $\rho_f$, and exploits it with Post-hoc Entropy Minimization. Whenever $\rho_f(0.99)$ clears the threshold, as for all three BCP checkpoints ($0.894$--$0.948$) but not the supervised baseline ($0.552$), a few minutes of label-free fine-tuning reduces HD95 by $27$--$43\%$ and improves Dice by $+0.6$ to $+1.3$ points.

PEM has two predictable failure modes, both diagnosable from a single label-free forward pass: diffuse residual entropy ($\rho_f\lesssim0.6$, the supervised checkpoint, where PEM reduces Dice by $6.25$ points) and vanishing residual entropy ($\rho_f\to1$, the saturated LA 10\% setting, where pseudo-label fine-tuning becomes competitive).

\noindent\textbf{Practical guidance.} PEM is a decision rule, not a universal step. Given a released checkpoint, $\rho_f(0.99)$ is computed from one label-free forward pass; if it exceeds $\rho^\star$ and the base Dice leaves headroom, the checkpoint is fine-tuned with the confident-shell loss at a learning rate in $[5\times10^{-6},10^{-5}]$ for a few epochs, the gain tracking the residual entropy budget: largest when $\rho_f$ is high yet accuracy imperfect (Pancreas 20\%, LA 5\%), smallest once saturated (LA 10\%).

The main limitation is that the diagnostic is validated on four checkpoints, so $\rho^\star$ and the $\rho_f$-to-gain relationship are coarse and the single supervised negative is confounded by training procedure. Densifying this is the key next experiment: a sweep of BCP checkpoints across training lengths, plus public SSL methods (UA-MT~\cite{yu2019uamt}, DTC~\cite{luo2022dtc}, MC-Net+~\cite{wu2022mcnet}, CPS~\cite{chen2021cps}) across label fractions, yields a family over which $\Delta$Dice regresses on $\rho_f$; multi-seed BCP retraining is the other open item.
