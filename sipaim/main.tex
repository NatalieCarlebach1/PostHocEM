\begin{abstract}
Semi-supervised learning (SSL) methods for medical image segmentation stop using the unlabeled data once training converges, even though the model's predictions on it still carry residual uncertainty concentrated at organ boundaries. We show that this residual entropy is spatially structured and exploit it with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization} (PEM): any converged SSL checkpoint is fine-tuned for two to five epochs with a single entropy-minimization loss on unlabeled data, using no labels, no pseudo-labels, and no architectural changes. We give a label-free diagnostic $\rho_f(\tau)$ that predicts in advance when PEM will help, and a gradient analysis of why the loss needs no masking in this regime. Applied to the publicly released Bidirectional Copy-Paste (BCP) checkpoints with no test-set tuning, PEM improves the Dice score by $+1.12$, $+1.27$, and $+0.62$ points on Pancreas-CT (20\% labeled), Left Atrium (5\% labeled), and Left Atrium (10\% labeled), respectively, and reduces the 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance by $27$--$43\%$, in under five minutes per run on a single GPU. Against three controlled post-hoc baselines --- temperature scaling, pseudo-label fine-tuning, and self-consistency --- PEM obtains the largest mean gain on the two less-saturated configurations, and every gain passes a per-case Wilcoxon signed-rank test ($p<0.025$). Applied instead to a supervised-only checkpoint, whose entropy is diffuse, PEM reduces Dice, exactly as the diagnostic predicts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Manual voxel-level annotation of 3D medical scans is slow and expensive, which makes semi-supervised learning (SSL) --- training from a small labeled set together with a large unlabeled set --- a practical necessity for volumetric segmentation. Recent SSL methods for this task~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher,yu2019uamt,bai2023bcp,liu2024most,assefa2025dycon} reach strong accuracy by using the unlabeled data during training and then discarding it at convergence.

We make a complementary observation: at convergence, a well-trained SSL model still assigns non-trivial prediction entropy to the unlabeled volumes, concentrated in a thin shell of low-confidence voxels on the predicted organ boundary. This concentration is a property of SSL convergence, not of any single method, and it is exactly the condition under which a label-free sharpening step is well-behaved --- the gradient it produces is already aligned with the locus of segmentation error.

We exploit this with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization} (PEM): any converged SSL checkpoint is fine-tuned for a few epochs on the unlabeled data with a single entropy objective --- no labels, pseudo-labels, thresholds, or architectural changes --- in minutes on one GPU, on top of a released checkpoint without retraining. Entropy minimization itself is classical~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy,sohn2020fixmatch}; our contribution is identifying its correct \textit{operating regime}, a converged optimum whose residual entropy is already concentrated, where the same loss needs no masking, curriculum, or class-balance regularizer.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item We introduce a label-free diagnostic, the \textit{shell entropy concentration} $\rho_f(\tau)$, that measures what fraction of a model's total prediction entropy is carried by its low-confidence voxels, and we use it to characterize when post-hoc sharpening is expected to help.
\item We propose PEM, a simple post-hoc fine-tuning step that improves any converged SSL checkpoint from unlabeled data alone, and we give a gradient analysis relating its behavior to the cluster assumption at the organ surface, which predicts both of its failure modes.
\item On three public SSL checkpoints spanning two organs and two modalities, PEM consistently improves overlap and boundary metrics, outperforms three controlled post-hoc baselines under an identical fixed-budget protocol with no test-set tuning, and fails only where the diagnostic predicts it should.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\noindent\textbf{SSL for medical image segmentation.}
Two mechanisms dominate: \emph{consistency regularization}, where a student network is trained to agree with a teacher or with itself under perturbations, and \emph{pseudo-labeling}, where confident predictions become hard targets. The Mean Teacher framework~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher} uses an exponential-moving-average (EMA) teacher; Uncertainty-Aware Mean Teacher (UA-MT)~\cite{yu2019uamt} filters the consistency target by estimated uncertainty; DTC~\cite{luo2022dtc} and MC-Net+~\cite{wu2022mcnet} enforce consistency across multiple heads or decoders; and Cross Pseudo Supervision (CPS)~\cite{chen2021cps} between two networks combines both mechanisms. Bidirectional Copy-Paste (BCP)~\cite{bai2023bcp} mixes labeled and unlabeled crops in both directions and is a strong, widely used baseline. MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} improve accuracy further but release training code only. All of these methods share a training-time contract: the unlabeled data is used during optimization and never revisited at convergence. PEM is the first to ask what that discarded data still contains and to turn the answer into a refinement step.

\noindent\textbf{Entropy minimization.}
Entropy minimization~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} adds $-\lambda H(Y\mid X)$ on unlabeled data and implements the low-density-separation principle of the cluster assumption~\cite{chapelle2006ssl}; Pseudo-Label~\cite{lee2013pseudolabel} is its hard-target limit, and modern methods such as FixMatch~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch} sharpen predictions to form pseudo-targets. In all of these the entropy term is active \emph{during} training, coupled to a supervised or consistency loss that prevents posterior collapse, and requires careful balancing over time. PEM uses the same formal loss but in a different regime: applied to an already-converged checkpoint, the residual entropy it sees is concentrated at boundaries, so the gradient is self-localized and none of the usual stabilizing machinery is needed.

\noindent\textbf{Test-time adaptation.}
The closest methodology is test-time adaptation. TENT~\cite{wang2021tent} minimizes prediction entropy on a test stream by updating only normalization parameters, and related methods use auxiliary objectives or continual updates. The loss is shared, but the problem is not: test-time adaptation assumes a \emph{distribution shift} and recovers accuracy lost to it, updating a subset of parameters against a moving test stream. PEM makes no shift assumption, operates on training-distribution unlabeled data, updates all parameters, and yields a single frozen-weights checkpoint evaluated on the benchmark's test set.

\noindent\textbf{Post-hoc calibration and refinement.}
Temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration} rescales logits by a scalar and, being monotone, cannot change the argmax and hence cannot move any overlap metric; we include it to rule out recalibration. Stochastic weight averaging~\cite{izmailov2018swa} and self-distillation~\cite{hinton2015distillation} refine a model without new labels but need the training trajectory or hard-target distillation, closer to our pseudo-label baseline.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Shell Entropy Concentration}
Let $f_{\theta^*}$ be a converged checkpoint and $\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}$ an unlabeled volume. For voxel $i$ with per-class softmax probabilities $p_{i,c}$, define the per-voxel entropy $H_i=-\sum_c p_{i,c}\log p_{i,c}$ and confidence $c_i=\max_c p_{i,c}$. The \textit{shell entropy concentration} at confidence threshold $\tau$ is
\begin{equation}
\rho_f(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\,\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau]\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\right]} \in [0,1].
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
$\rho_f(\tau)$ is the fraction of total prediction entropy carried by voxels of confidence at most $\tau$. It is label-free, scalar, and self-localizing: on a converged network the low-confidence voxels lie near the decision boundary. We use $\tau=0.99$ throughout and treat $\rho^\star=0.85$ as a suitability threshold, above which post-hoc sharpening is expected to help.

\subsection{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}
PEM fine-tunes the checkpoint on the unlabeled set with the entropy loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{PEM}} \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}}\frac{\sum_{i} m_i H_i}{\sum_i m_i+\epsilon},
\qquad m_i=\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau],
\label{eq:pem}
\end{equation}
optionally restricted to the confidence shell through the mask $m_i$. No labels or pseudo-labels are used. Fine-tuning runs for a small, fixed number of epochs $E$ with a small learning rate.

\section{Analysis: Why PEM Works}
\label{sec:analysis}
Consider a binary voxel with logit $z$ and probability $p=\sigma(z)$. Its per-voxel entropy gradient is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial H(p(z))}{\partial z} \;=\; \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\cdot\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} \;=\; (-z)\cdot p(1-p) \;=\; -\,z\,p(1-p).
\label{eq:dHdz}
\end{equation}
The gradient is the product of the logit $z$, large where the network is confident, and the variance factor $p(1-p)$, large only where it is undecided; the product peaks at intermediate logits ($|z|\approx1.54$) and vanishes at both extremes.

\begin{proposition}[Implicit boundary-shell gating]
\label{prop:gate}
For the unmasked PEM loss ($\tau{=}1$), the magnitude of each voxel's gradient contribution is proportional to $|z_i|\,p_i(1{-}p_i)$, which vanishes as $p_i\to0$ or $p_i\to1$. The loss is therefore dominated by the uncertain shell $\{i:p_i\approx0.5\}$, and no explicit confidence mask is required for it to localize at the decision surface.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:gate} has three consequences. \emph{(i) No class-balance term is needed}: a voxel already saturated by SSL training has $p(1{-}p)\approx0$ and cannot be driven further, so a converged checkpoint cannot collapse to a single class --- the classical objection to unmasked entropy minimization~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} does not apply post-hoc. \emph{(ii) The gradient shrinks as the model saturates}: with $\bar V=\mathbb{E}_i[p_i(1{-}p_i)]$, the update scales as $O(\bar V)$, so as $\rho_f(0.99)\to1$ and $\bar V\to0$ the gains diminish and pseudo-label fine-tuning, whose gradient is $O(1)$, becomes competitive. \emph{(iii) Boundary refinement vs.\ relocation}: on a boundary voxel the step has the sign of $z_i$ and commits it to the currently more probable class. If SSL convergence placed the boundary in the right neighborhood, PEM refines it; if not (a supervised-only checkpoint with diffuse $\rho_f$), it reinforces the error.

Because partial-volume averaging and weak soft-tissue contrast make exactly the anatomical-boundary voxels ambiguous in feature space, the network's decision surface co-locates with the physical organ surface in 3D segmentation. Entropy minimization there implements the cluster assumption~\cite{chapelle2006ssl,grandvalet2004entropy} as boundary refinement, and $\rho_f$ measures whether the uncertain voxels form the thin physical shell (refinement) or are scattered through the interior (relocation).

\section{Datasets and Implementation}
\label{sec:data}
\noindent\textbf{Datasets.}
We evaluate on two public benchmarks. Pancreas-CT~\cite{roth2015pancreas} contains abdominal Computed Tomography (CT) scans; following the standard split we use 12 labeled, 50 unlabeled, and 18 test cases (20\% labeled). The Left Atrium (LA) 2018 dataset contains cardiac Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) scans; we use the standard 5\% (4 labeled) and 10\% (8 labeled) splits with 20 test cases. Both use the preprocessing distributed with BCP~\cite{bai2023bcp}.

\noindent\textbf{Checkpoints and protocol.}
We start from the publicly released BCP checkpoints (V-Net backbone; instance normalization for Pancreas, batch normalization for LA). PEM uses Adam (no weight decay, no schedule) with gradient clipping at $1$ and learning rates of $5\times10^{-5}$ (Pancreas) and $1\times10^{-5}$ (LA), selected by 5-fold cross-validation on the labeled folds only; LA batch-normalization statistics are frozen. The epoch budget is fixed \textit{a priori} at $E=2$ (Pancreas) and $E=5$ (LA). Reported PEM numbers are the mean over seeds $\{2020,42,123\}$. Inference uses sliding windows with BCP's original strides and no test-time augmentation (TTA); each PEM run completes in under $5.2$ minutes on a single RTX 4070 Ti SUPER.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics and post-hoc baselines.}
We report the Dice score and Jaccard index (higher is better), and the 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance (HD95) and Average Surface Distance (ASD) in voxels (lower is better). Three post-hoc baselines receive the same checkpoint, budget, learning rate, and data:
\textit{Temperature Scaling} (TS) fits a scalar temperature on unlabeled entropy and cannot change the argmax;
\textit{Pseudo-Label Fine-Tuning} (PL-FT) generates hard pseudo-labels (argmax then largest connected component, CC) and fine-tunes with cross-entropy plus Dice;
\textit{Self-Consistency} (SC) minimizes the mean-squared error between the student on a noised input and the frozen teacher on the clean input.
We compare against BCP because MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} release training code only.

\noindent\textbf{The diagnostic is predictive.}
Applying PEM to the supervised checkpoint ($\rho_f=0.552$, below $\rho^\star$) \emph{reduces} its Dice from $75.66$ to $69.41$ ($-6.25$ points) within one epoch: when the uncertain voxels do not form a boundary shell, sharpening reinforces errors, exactly as consequence (iii) predicts. A practitioner can thus run this label-free check before committing to PEM.

\noindent\textbf{PEM improves every SSL checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab:main}).}
TS produces $|\Delta\text{Dice}|\le0.03$ everywhere, confirming that PEM's gain comes from parameter updates at boundary voxels rather than recalibration; we omit its row. PEM attains the largest Dice and HD95 improvement on the two less-saturated configurations ($+1.12$ and $+1.27$ Dice, versus $+0.50$ and $+0.90$ for PL-FT); on the most saturated setting (LA 10\%) PL-FT marginally exceeds PEM ($+0.68$ versus $+0.62$) with overlapping bootstrap intervals, consistent with the $p(1{-}p)$ gradient shrinking as $\rho_f\to1$ [consequence (ii)]. \noindent\textbf{Mask and threshold ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).}
Restricting the loss to the confidence shell helps: the confident-shell mask beats the unmasked (full) loss at every learning rate, with a sweet spot near $\tau=0.95$, and the gain grows by $30$--$60\%$ from LR $5\times10^{-6}$ to $10^{-5}$ (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}); $\tau=0.999$ discards the shell and $\tau=0.90$ admits saturated noise. The learning rate has a hard floor and ceiling: below $10^{-6}$ no update occurs (all deltas $0.0$), while above $5\times10^{-5}$ the boundary is relocated rather than refined. We fix $\tau=0.99$ \textit{a priori}; the ablation shows a tuned $\tau=0.95$ would yield a larger gain ($+1.61$ on LA 5\%), which we do not claim, to avoid test-set tuning. Two design choices are confirmed on Pancreas-CT: (a) on a converged checkpoint the student and its EMA teacher agree almost everywhere, so a disagreement mask~\cite{yu2019uamt} is nearly empty and yields no update --- the confidence shell is the correct post-hoc mask; and (b) updating only the decoder or the EMA weights gives $\le0$ gain, so PEM updates all student parameters (unlike normalization-only TTA~\cite{wang2021tent}). Dice is monotonic in $E$ up to a peak at $E=8$; we report the more conservative \textit{a priori} $E=5$.

\noindent\textbf{Convergence is immediate.}
Most of the gain arrives in the first epoch --- HD95 drops sharply at epoch~$1$ (e.g.\ $13.9\!\to\!8.4$ voxels on LA 5\%) while Dice rises monotonically and plateaus within the budget --- confirming that $E\!\in\!\{2,5\}$ suffices and the gain is a stable refinement, not a lucky early stop.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We showed that converged SSL checkpoints leave a structured, boundary-localized residual entropy on their unlabeled data, quantified it with the label-free diagnostic $\rho_f(\tau)$, and exploited it with Post-hoc Entropy Minimization. Whenever $\rho_f(0.99)$ clears the threshold --- as it does for all three released BCP checkpoints ($0.894$--$0.948$) but not for a supervised baseline ($0.552$) --- a few minutes of label-free fine-tuning improves Dice by $+0.6$ to $+1.3$ points and reduces HD95 by $27$--$43\%$.

PEM has two predictable failure modes, both diagnosable from a single label-free forward pass and both borne out above: diffuse residual entropy ($\rho_f\lesssim0.6$, as on the supervised checkpoint, where PEM reduces Dice by $6.25$ points), and vanishing residual entropy ($\rho_f\to1$, as on the saturated LA 10\% setting, where pseudo-label fine-tuning becomes competitive).

\noindent\textbf{Practical guidance.} PEM is a decision rule, not a universal step. Given a released checkpoint, compute $\rho_f(0.99)$ from one label-free forward pass over the unlabeled set; if it exceeds $\rho^\star$ and the base Dice still leaves headroom, fine-tune with the confident-shell loss at a learning rate in $[5\times10^{-6},10^{-5}]$ for a few epochs. The expected gain tracks the residual entropy budget: largest when $\rho_f$ is high yet accuracy is imperfect (Pancreas 20\%, LA 5\%) and smallest once the checkpoint has saturated (LA 10\%).

A limitation of the present study is that our head-to-head comparison is against BCP, since stronger recent methods release training code only; extending PEM across additional released checkpoints is left to future work.
