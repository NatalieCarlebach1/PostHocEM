\begin{abstract}
Semi-supervised learning methods for medical image segmentation, or SSL methods, stop using the unlabeled data once training converges, even though the model's predictions on it still carry residual uncertainty concentrated at organ boundaries. This paper shows that this residual entropy is spatially structured and exploits it with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}, or PEM: any converged SSL checkpoint is fine-tuned for two to five epochs with a single entropy-minimization loss on unlabeled data, using no labels, no pseudo-labels, and no architectural changes. A label-free diagnostic $\rho_f$ predicts in advance when PEM will help, and a gradient analysis explains why the loss localizes to the boundary with no explicit mask, and why masking nonetheless helps, through the loss normalization rather than the gating. In a corrected retrospective reanalysis, the learning rate and mask threshold are chosen on the labeled training volumes, which no post-hoc objective sees, rather than on test data. Applied to the publicly released Bidirectional Copy-Paste, or BCP, checkpoints, PEM reduces the 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance by $25$ to $44\%$ and improves the Dice score by $+1.02$, $+0.67$, and $+0.62$ points on Pancreas-CT at 20\% labeled, Left Atrium at 5\% labeled, and Left Atrium at 10\% labeled, in minutes per run on a single GPU. Against temperature scaling, pseudo-label fine-tuning, and self-consistency under an identical protocol and selection rule, the comparison separates by metric rather than by dataset: PEM leads both boundary metrics wherever the checkpoint retains headroom, by a $23\%$ margin in HD95, and all four on Pancreas-CT, while on Left Atrium PEM and pseudo-label fine-tuning are statistically indistinguishable in Dice. A mask ablation at a fixed budget identifies one threshold that degrades none of the checkpoints tested, where looser ones cost up to $30$ Dice points.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Manual voxel-level annotation of 3D medical scans is slow and expensive, which makes semi-supervised learning, or SSL, training from a small labeled set together with a large unlabeled set, a practical necessity for volumetric segmentation. Recent SSL methods for this task~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher,yu2019uamt,bai2023bcp,liu2024most,assefa2025dycon} reach strong accuracy by using the unlabeled data during training and then discarding it at convergence.

This paper makes a complementary observation: at convergence a well-trained SSL model still assigns non-trivial prediction entropy to the unlabeled volumes, concentrated in a thin shell of low-confidence voxels on the predicted organ boundary. The concentration is a property of SSL convergence rather than of any single method, and it is exactly the condition under which a label-free sharpening step is well-behaved, the gradient it produces being already aligned with the locus of segmentation error. This is exploited with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}, or PEM: a converged checkpoint is fine-tuned for a few epochs on the unlabeled data with a single entropy objective, using no labels, pseudo-labels or architectural changes, in minutes on one GPU. Entropy minimization is classical~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} and standard in modern SSL~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch}; what is new is its operating regime and the three results that follow.

\noindent\textbf{A label-free diagnostic.}
The first contribution is the \textit{shell entropy concentration} $\rho_f$, a scalar that turns the observation above into a test. For a converged checkpoint it is the fraction of total prediction entropy carried by the low-confidence voxels of the unlabeled set, so it needs neither labels nor a validation set and follows from a single forward pass. It separates checkpoints on which post-hoc sharpening is well-conditioned from those on which it is not: the three released BCP checkpoints hold $89$ to $95\%$ of their entropy in a shell of a few percent of the voxels, whereas a supervised-only network on the same labels keeps $55\%$ there and scatters the rest through the interior (Table~\ref{tab:entropy}).

\noindent\textbf{PEM and why it works.}
The second contribution is PEM together with an account of why the classical loss needs no stabilizers here. The entropy gradient is supported on a band of moderate confidence flanking the boundary and vanishes both on the saturated interior and at maximum ambiguity, so it gates itself to the shell with no explicit mask; the confidence mask instead acts through the loss normalization, which is what makes it insurance against collapse rather than a source of gain. Because partial-volume averaging and weak soft-tissue contrast make the anatomical-boundary voxels the ambiguous ones, the feature-space decision surface co-locates with the physical organ surface, so entropy minimization there realizes the cluster assumption as boundary refinement, and the same argument predicts the two regimes in which PEM should fail.

\noindent\textbf{A corrected, retrospective evaluation.}
The third contribution is an empirical study over two organs and two modalities, reanalysed so that learning rates and thresholds are no longer chosen on test data. Because no post-hoc method here optimizes on labels, the labeled training volumes, held out from the post-hoc step though not from base training, serve as the selection set, and the identical rule is applied to PEM and to every baseline. Under it PEM improves all four metrics on all three released BCP checkpoints and leads both boundary metrics wherever the checkpoint retains headroom (Table~\ref{tab:main}), although on Left Atrium PEM and pseudo-label fine-tuning are not statistically separable in Dice. The ablation of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, read at a fixed budget, identifies the one threshold that degrades none of the checkpoints tested, and at that threshold PEM degrades none of the checkpoints clearing the suitability threshold, although looser configurations can.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\noindent\textbf{SSL for medical image segmentation.}
Two mechanisms dominate: \emph{consistency regularization}, where a student agrees with a teacher or itself under perturbations, and \emph{pseudo-labeling}, where confident predictions become hard targets. Mean Teacher~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher} uses an EMA teacher; UA-MT~\cite{yu2019uamt} filters by uncertainty; DTC~\cite{luo2022dtc} and MC-Net+~\cite{wu2022mcnet} enforce consistency across heads or decoders; CPS~\cite{chen2021cps} combines both; BCP~\cite{bai2023bcp} mixes labeled and unlabeled crops bidirectionally, while MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} improve further but release training code only. All share a training-time contract: the unlabeled data is used during optimization and never revisited. PEM revisits what it still contains at convergence; the novelty is not entropy minimization but characterizing the regime, with a label-free diagnostic, in which it is well-conditioned.

\noindent\textbf{Entropy minimization and test-time adaptation.}
Entropy minimization~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} adds $-\lambda H(Y\mid X)$ on unlabeled data, implementing low-density separation~\cite{chapelle2006ssl}; Pseudo-Label~\cite{lee2013pseudolabel} is its hard-target limit and FixMatch~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch} sharpens predictions into pseudo-targets. In all of these the term is active \emph{during} training, coupled to a loss preventing posterior collapse and needing balancing over time. The closest methodology is test-time adaptation: TENT~\cite{wang2021tent} minimizes entropy on a test stream updating only normalization parameters, SHOT~\cite{liang2020shot} adds information maximization, and EATA~\cite{niu2022eata} and SAR~\cite{niu2023sar} add entropy-based selection, the same masking question as Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, documenting the collapse unrestricted minimization can cause. All assume distribution shift and adapt a parameter subset against a moving target. PEM uses the same loss in a different regime: on a converged checkpoint and training-distribution data the residual entropy is already boundary-concentrated, so the gradient is self-localized, all parameters can be updated, and the collapse these methods guard against is bounded away (Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}).

\noindent\textbf{Post-hoc calibration and refinement.}
Temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration} rescales logits by a scalar and, being monotone, cannot change the argmax and hence cannot move any overlap metric; it is included to rule out recalibration. Stochastic weight averaging~\cite{izmailov2018swa} and self-distillation~\cite{hinton2015distillation} refine a model without new labels but need the training trajectory or hard-target distillation, closer to the pseudo-label baseline.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Shell Entropy Concentration}
Let $f_{\theta^*}$ be a converged checkpoint and $\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}$ an unlabeled volume. For voxel $i$ with per-class softmax probabilities $p_{i,c}$, define the per-voxel entropy $H_i=-\sum_c p_{i,c}\log p_{i,c}$ and confidence $c_i=\max_c p_{i,c}$. The \textit{shell entropy concentration} at confidence threshold $\tau$ is
\begin{equation}
\rho_f(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\,\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau]\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\right]} \in [0,1].
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
$\rho_f(\tau)$ is the fraction of total prediction entropy carried by voxels of confidence at most $\tau$. It is label-free, scalar and self-localizing, since on a converged network the low-confidence voxels lie near the decision boundary. It is always evaluated at $\tau=0.99$, with $\rho^\star=0.85$ a provisional suitability threshold placed below the SSL checkpoints and well above the supervised baseline of Table~\ref{tab:entropy}. The loss below uses a \emph{separate} threshold $\tau_m$: $\tau$ measures where the entropy sits, $\tau_m$ decides which voxels the loss averages over.

\subsection{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}
PEM fine-tunes the checkpoint on the unlabeled set with the entropy loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{PEM}} \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}}\frac{\sum_{i} m_i H_i}{\sum_i m_i+\epsilon},
\qquad m_i=\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau_m],
\label{eq:pem}
\end{equation}
restricted to the confidence shell through $m_i$, with $\epsilon$ guarding the empty-mask case. No labels or pseudo-labels are used, and fine-tuning runs for a small fixed $E$ at a small learning rate.

\section{Analysis: Why PEM Works}
\label{sec:analysis}
\textit{The gradient.} The analysis is stated for a binary voxel with logit $z$ and probability $p=\sigma(z)$; the multi-class case replaces $z\,p(1-p)$ with the analogous per-logit softmax term. The per-voxel entropy gradient is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial H(p(z))}{\partial z} \;=\; \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\cdot\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} \;=\; (-z)\cdot p(1-p) \;=\; -\,z\,p(1-p).
\label{eq:dHdz}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Boundary-band gradient support]
\label{prop:gate}
For the unmasked entropy loss, the magnitude of each voxel's gradient is proportional to $|z_i|\,p_i(1{-}p_i)$, which vanishes at $p_i\to0$, at $p_i\to1$, and at $p_i=0.5$, and is maximized at $|z_i|\approx1.54$. The gradient is thus automatically supported on the moderate-confidence band flanking the decision surface, with no explicit confidence mask.
\end{proposition}

\textit{Gating versus normalization.} Proposition~\ref{prop:gate} fixes which voxels receive a gradient but not the step size, and the two reconcile Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. Gating is automatic and the mask leaves it unchanged; what the mask changes is the normalization, since Eq.~\ref{eq:pem} is a masked mean over $\sum_i m_i$ whereas unmasked it averages over a volume that is $91$ to $95\%$ saturated, diluting the shell by $15$ to $20\times$. What this buys is asymmetric. On the instance-normalized Pancreas checkpoint, which is robust, the unmasked loss is in fact better, by $0.12$ and $0.08$ Dice at the two learning rates; on the batch-normalized LA checkpoints, which are brittle, dilution is the difference between a gain and a collapse, costing up to $11$ Dice points unmasked and $30$ at the loosest threshold. The mask is therefore insurance rather than a source of gain.

\textit{Two bounded properties.} \emph{(i)} At small learning rate and high $\rho_f$ sharpening does not collapse a converged checkpoint, the gradient vanishing on committed voxels; the bound weakens as $\rho_f$ falls or the rate grows, where PEM degrades the checkpoint. \emph{(ii)} The mean gradient scales with $\bar V=\mathbb{E}_i[p_i(1{-}p_i)]$, so as $\rho_f\to1$ and $\bar V\to0$ the gain diminishes and pseudo-label fine-tuning, whose gradient does not vanish on committed voxels, becomes competitive.

\textit{Refinement, not relocation.} The step has the sign of $z_i$, committing a boundary voxel to its more probable class, so it refines a boundary that SSL convergence placed correctly and reinforces the error otherwise. This is why the effect is largest on the surface metrics and why $\rho_f$, which measures whether the uncertain voxels form a shell or scatter through the interior, is the quantity that decides in advance which of the two happens~\cite{chapelle2006ssl,grandvalet2004entropy}.

\section{Datasets and Implementation}
\label{sec:data}
\noindent\textbf{Datasets.}
Pancreas-CT~\cite{roth2015pancreas} (abdominal CT) is split into 12 labeled, 50 unlabeled and 18 test cases (20\% labeled); Left Atrium (LA) 2018 (cardiac MRI) at 5\% (4 labeled) and 10\% (8 labeled) with 20 test cases. Both use the preprocessing distributed with BCP~\cite{bai2023bcp}.

\noindent\textbf{Checkpoints and protocol.}
Experiments start from the publicly released BCP checkpoints (V-Net; instance normalization for Pancreas, batch normalization for LA). PEM uses Adam with gradient clipping at $1$, no weight decay or schedule, and LA batch-normalization statistics frozen. The budgets $E=2$ (Pancreas) and $E=5$ (LA) are retained from the submitted version, where they were set with test-set learning curves in view; they are not re-selected, and the final epoch is reported. All numbers are means over seeds $\{2020,42,123\}$. Inference uses sliding windows with BCP's strides and no test-time augmentation; each run takes under $5.2$ minutes on one RTX 4070 Ti SUPER.

\noindent\textbf{Selection protocol.}
No post-hoc method here optimizes on labels, so the labeled training volumes ($12$ Pancreas, $4$ and $8$ LA) are never seen by any of them and serve as the set for choosing the learning rate and mask threshold, held out from the post-hoc step though not from base training. This is not cross-validation: no fold is trained on, because no fold is ever used. Selection maximizes the Dice \emph{change} these volumes report, never their absolute Dice, which is optimistic because the base checkpoint was fitted on them. Checked retrospectively over $33$ configurations, the $30$ of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and three learning-rate-ceiling runs, that change ranks configurations as the test change does, with Spearman $\rho$ of $0.78$ (Pancreas), $0.75$ (LA 5\%) and $0.99$ (LA 10\%), and $0.67$ to $0.96$ once outright collapses are removed, so it is not merely separating collapse from non-collapse. The rule selects $\tau_m=0.90$ in all three settings, at $5\times10^{-5}$ (Pancreas) and $5\times10^{-6}$ (LA), and every baseline is put through it over the identical grid.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics and post-hoc baselines.}
Metrics are Dice and Jaccard (higher better) and HD95 and Average Surface Distance (ASD) in voxels (lower better). Three post-hoc baselines receive the same checkpoint, budget, data and selection rule: \textit{Temperature Scaling} (TS) fits a scalar temperature and cannot change the argmax; \textit{Pseudo-Label Fine-Tuning} (PL-FT) generates hard pseudo-labels (argmax then largest connected component) and fine-tunes with cross-entropy plus Dice; \textit{Self-Consistency} (SC) minimizes the squared error between the student on a noised input and the frozen teacher on the clean one. The comparison is against BCP because MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} release training code only.

\noindent\textbf{PEM refines boundaries (Table~\ref{tab:main}).}
TS moves Dice by at most $0.03$, so its row is omitted: none of this is recalibration, a monotone rescaling being unable to move an argmax. PEM improves every metric on all three checkpoints, by $+1.02$, $+0.67$ and $+0.62$ Dice and by $24.9\%$, $44.0\%$ and $30.5\%$ of HD95, stably across seeds ($\pm0.05$, $\pm0.15$, $\pm0.06$). Against the other post-hoc methods the comparison separates by metric rather than by setting. PEM is best on the boundary metrics wherever the checkpoint retains headroom, with HD95 $5.87$ against $7.62$ for the nearest competitor on Pancreas and $7.79$ against $10.11$ on LA 5\%, a $23\%$ margin in both, and ASD following; on Dice it leads only on Pancreas, whose base checkpoint is furthest from saturation. That split is what a boundary-refinement step should produce, HD95 and ASD measuring surface agreement where the gradient acts, Dice being volumetric and dominated by the interior PEM leaves untouched. The two also move on different timescales: on LA 5\% HD95 falls from $13.91$ to $9.18$ voxels in one epoch, two thirds of its total gain, while Dice dips to $87.11$ before recovering to $88.03$ by epoch~$5$. Both curves are flat by the end of the budget, and the reported epoch is the last rather than the best.

\noindent\textbf{What is, and is not, significant.}
Per-case paired tests ($N{=}18$ Pancreas, $N{=}20$ LA) qualify these gains. Against BCP, PEM is significant on Pancreas ($+1.04$, $p{=}.030$) and LA 10\% ($+0.63$, $p{=}.030$) but \emph{not} on LA 5\% ($+0.70$, $p{=}.097$). Head to head, PEM leads PL-FT on Pancreas ($+0.74$, CI $[+0.10,+1.39]$, $p{=}.081$) and is indistinguishable from it on LA 5\% ($-0.26$, $p{=}.729$) and LA 10\% ($-0.07$, $p{=}.674$), so PL-FT's nominal LA lead is not evidence that pseudo-labelling is better there; twenty cases do not separate the three. PL-FT is the more \emph{consistent} method, beating BCP on $16$ to $18$ of $20$ cases where PEM wins $12$ to $14$, a difference of variance rather than mean. PEM's Dice margin over PL-FT tracks headroom, $+0.73$, $-0.25$ and $-0.07$ at base Dice $82.89$, $87.32$ and $89.40$, which is consequence (ii) of Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis} measured: the entropy gradient scales with $\bar V$ and vanishes as predictions commit, the pseudo-label gradient does not.



\noindent\textbf{What the qualitative maps show (Fig.~\ref{fig:qual}).}
The entropy column shows the per-voxel entropy $H_i$ that $\rho_f$ summarizes: a thin bright rim on the predicted surface over an interior dark almost everywhere, and it is that rim the gradient acts on. The correction column is the more informative, and is not uniformly favourable. Corrections of either sign lie on the surface rather than the interior in all three settings, which is the mechanism's claim, but their balance tracks headroom: green outnumbers red along the rim on LA 5\%, corrections are sparse but boundary-located on Pancreas, and on the saturated LA 10\% red covers part of the rim even in a case PEM improves overall, a reminder that at $\rho_f\to1$ the step may reinforce an error as easily as fix one.

\noindent\textbf{Mask and threshold ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).}
Read at the fixed budget, the ablation says how much of the mask's value is insurance. A tight $\tau_m=0.90$ isolates the boundary band while a loose $\tau_m=0.999$ readmits the interior and approaches the unmasked loss, and the count of degraded cells rises monotonically along that path, from $0$ to $3$; the failures are not small, $\tau_m=0.999$ costing $30$ Dice points on LA 10\% at $10^{-5}$. $\tau_m=0.95$ attains the largest single gain, but its advantage over $\tau_m=0.99$ is not stable at a fixed budget: it wins three of six cells and loses three, by $0.03$ to $0.06$ on Pancreas. $\tau_m=0.90$ is recommended instead, since it alone never degrades a checkpoint and it also has the highest mean over the six cells ($+0.72$ against $+0.46$). The learning rate has a floor and a ceiling: below $10^{-6}$ no update occurs, and at $5\times10^{-5}$ the batch-normalized LA checkpoints are destroyed outright, losing $80$ and $85$ Dice, while instance-normalized Pancreas is stable there. Two further choices are confirmed on Pancreas: a disagreement mask~\cite{yu2019uamt} is empty on a converged checkpoint, so the confidence shell is the right post-hoc mask; and decoder-only or EMA-only updates give $\le0$ gain, so PEM updates all student parameters, unlike normalization-only TTA~\cite{wang2021tent}.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Converged SSL checkpoints leave a structured, boundary-localized residual entropy; $\rho_f$ quantifies it without labels and PEM exploits it. Whenever $\rho_f(0.99)$ clears the threshold, as for all three BCP checkpoints but not the supervised baseline, minutes of label-free fine-tuning reduce HD95 by $25$ to $44\%$ and improve Dice by $+0.6$ to $+1.0$, the boundary metrics moving first and furthest. Both failure modes are diagnosable from one forward pass: diffuse entropy ($\rho_f\lesssim0.6$) and vanishing entropy ($\rho_f\to1$).

\noindent\textbf{Practical guidance.} Compute $\rho_f(0.99)$ from one label-free forward pass; if it clears $\rho^\star$ and the base Dice leaves headroom, fine-tune at $\tau_m=0.90$, the only threshold that degraded nothing tested here, and set the learning rate by normalization type rather than copying it across datasets: $5\times10^{-6}$ for batch-normalized checkpoints, destroyed outright at $5\times10^{-5}$, up to $5\times10^{-5}$ for instance-normalized ones.

Three limitations bound these claims: the budgets $E$ were retained from the original submission rather than re-selected; $\rho^\star$ rests on four checkpoints and so is coarse; and $18$ to $20$ test volumes separate PEM from pseudo-label fine-tuning in no setting at the $0.05$ level. A denser checkpoint sweep would address all three.
