\begin{abstract}
Semi-supervised learning methods for medical image segmentation, or SSL methods, stop using the unlabeled data once training converges, even though the model's predictions on it still carry residual uncertainty concentrated at organ boundaries. This paper shows that this residual entropy is spatially structured and exploits it with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}, or PEM: any converged SSL checkpoint is fine-tuned for two to five epochs with a single entropy-minimization loss on unlabeled data, using no labels, no pseudo-labels, and no architectural changes. A label-free diagnostic $\rho_f$, computed before fine-tuning, separates the checkpoints PEM improves from the one it destroys, and a gradient analysis explains why the loss localizes to the boundary with no explicit mask, and why masking nonetheless helps, through the loss normalization rather than the gating. In a corrected retrospective reanalysis, the learning rate and mask threshold are chosen on the labeled training volumes, which no post-hoc objective sees, rather than on test data. Applied to the publicly released Bidirectional Copy-Paste, or BCP, checkpoints, PEM reduces the 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance by $25$ to $44\%$ and improves the Dice score by $+1.02$, $+0.66$, and $+0.62$ points on Pancreas-CT at 20\% labeled, Left Atrium at 5\% labeled, and Left Atrium at 10\% labeled, in minutes per run on a single GPU. Against temperature scaling, pseudo-label fine-tuning, and self-consistency under an identical protocol and selection rule, the comparison separates by dataset: on Pancreas-CT PEM improves HD95 significantly over BCP and over both post-hoc baselines after Holm correction ($p\le.003$), while on Left Atrium its gains over BCP are nominal and it is statistically indistinguishable from pseudo-label fine-tuning on both metrics. A mask ablation at a fixed budget identifies one threshold that degrades none of the SSL checkpoints, where looser ones cost up to $30$ Dice points.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Manual voxel-level annotation of 3D medical scans is slow and expensive, which makes semi-supervised learning, or SSL, training from a small labeled set together with a large unlabeled set, a practical necessity for volumetric segmentation. Recent SSL methods for this task~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher,yu2019uamt,bai2023bcp,liu2024most,assefa2025dycon} reach strong accuracy by using the unlabeled data during training and then discarding it at convergence.

This paper makes a complementary observation: at convergence a well-trained SSL model still assigns non-trivial prediction entropy to the unlabeled volumes, concentrated in a thin shell of low-confidence voxels on the predicted organ boundary. The concentration is a property of SSL convergence rather than of any single method, and it is exactly the condition under which a label-free sharpening step is well-behaved, the gradient it produces being already aligned with the locus of segmentation error. This is exploited with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}, or PEM: a converged checkpoint is fine-tuned for a few epochs on the unlabeled data with a single entropy objective, using no labels, pseudo-labels or architectural changes, in minutes on one GPU. Entropy minimization is classical~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} and standard in modern SSL~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch}; what is new is its operating regime and the three results that follow.

\noindent\textbf{A label-free diagnostic.}
The first contribution is the \textit{shell entropy concentration} $\rho_f$, a scalar that turns the observation above into a test. For a converged checkpoint it is the fraction of total prediction entropy carried by the low-confidence voxels of the unlabeled set, so it needs neither labels nor a validation set and follows from a single forward pass. On the four checkpoints evaluated it separates those on which post-hoc sharpening is well-conditioned from the one on which it is not: the three released BCP checkpoints hold $89$ to $95\%$ of their entropy in a shell of a few percent of the voxels, whereas a supervised-only network on the same labels keeps $55\%$ there and scatters the rest through the interior (Table~\ref{tab:entropy}).

\noindent\textbf{PEM and why it works.}
The second contribution is PEM with an account of why the classical loss needs no stabilizers here. The entropy gradient is supported on a moderate-confidence band flanking the boundary and vanishes on the saturated interior and at maximum ambiguity, so it gates itself to the shell; the confidence mask acts through the loss normalization, as insurance against collapse rather than a source of gain. Because partial-volume averaging and weak soft-tissue contrast make anatomical-boundary voxels the ambiguous ones, the decision surface co-locates with the organ surface, so entropy minimization realizes the cluster assumption as boundary refinement, and the argument predicts two regimes in which PEM should fail.

\noindent\textbf{A corrected, retrospective evaluation.}
The third contribution is an empirical study over two organs and two modalities, reanalysed so that learning rates and thresholds are no longer chosen on test data: the labeled training volumes, held out from the post-hoc step though not from base training, serve as the selection set for PEM and every baseline alike. PEM improves all four metrics on all three released BCP checkpoints (Table~\ref{tab:main}); seed-averaged per-case tests find the boundary gain significant against every competitor on Pancreas-CT but not on Left Atrium, where PEM and pseudo-label fine-tuning are not separable (Table~\ref{tab:sig}). The selected configuration degrades none of the SSL checkpoints and destroys the one below the suitability threshold.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\noindent\textbf{SSL for medical image segmentation.}
Two mechanisms dominate: \emph{consistency regularization}, where a student agrees with a teacher or itself under perturbations, and \emph{pseudo-labeling}, where confident predictions become hard targets. Mean Teacher~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher} uses an EMA teacher; UA-MT~\cite{yu2019uamt} filters by uncertainty; DTC~\cite{luo2022dtc} and MC-Net+~\cite{wu2022mcnet} enforce consistency across heads or decoders; CPS~\cite{chen2021cps} combines both; BCP~\cite{bai2023bcp} mixes labeled and unlabeled crops bidirectionally, while MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} improve further but release training code only. All share a training-time contract: the unlabeled data is used during optimization and never revisited. PEM revisits what it still contains at convergence; the novelty is not entropy minimization but characterizing the regime, with a label-free diagnostic, in which it is well-conditioned.

\noindent\textbf{Entropy minimization and test-time adaptation.}
Entropy minimization~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} implements low-density separation~\cite{chapelle2006ssl}; Pseudo-Label~\cite{lee2013pseudolabel} is its hard-target limit and FixMatch~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch} sharpens predictions into pseudo-targets, all \emph{during} training and coupled to a loss preventing collapse. The closest methodology is test-time adaptation: TENT~\cite{wang2021tent} minimizes entropy on a test stream updating only normalization parameters, SHOT~\cite{liang2020shot} adds information maximization, and EATA~\cite{niu2022eata} and SAR~\cite{niu2023sar} add entropy-based selection, the masking question of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, documenting the collapse unrestricted minimization can cause. All assume distribution shift. PEM uses the same loss on a converged checkpoint and training-distribution data, where the residual entropy is already boundary-concentrated, so all parameters can be updated.

\noindent\textbf{Post-hoc refinement.}
Temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration} is monotone and cannot move an argmax, so it is included only to rule out recalibration; stochastic weight averaging~\cite{izmailov2018swa} and self-distillation~\cite{hinton2015distillation} need the training trajectory or hard targets, closer to the pseudo-label baseline.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Shell Entropy Concentration}
Let $f_{\theta^*}$ be a converged checkpoint and $\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}$ an unlabeled volume. For voxel $i$ with per-class softmax probabilities $p_{i,c}$, define the per-voxel entropy $H_i=-\sum_c p_{i,c}\log p_{i,c}$ and confidence $c_i=\max_c p_{i,c}$. The \textit{shell entropy concentration} at confidence threshold $\tau$ is
\begin{equation}
\rho_f(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\,\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau]\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\right]} \in [0,1].
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
$\rho_f(\tau)$ is the fraction of total prediction entropy carried by voxels of confidence at most $\tau$. It is label-free, scalar and self-localizing, since on a converged network the low-confidence voxels lie near the decision boundary. It is always evaluated at $\tau=0.99$, with $\rho^\star=0.85$ a provisional suitability threshold placed below the SSL checkpoints and well above the supervised baseline of Table~\ref{tab:entropy}. The loss below uses a \emph{separate} threshold $\tau_m$: $\tau$ measures where the entropy sits, $\tau_m$ decides which voxels the loss averages over.

\subsection{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}
PEM fine-tunes the checkpoint on the unlabeled set with the entropy loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{PEM}} \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}}\frac{\sum_{i} m_i H_i}{\sum_i m_i+\epsilon},
\qquad m_i=\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau_m],
\label{eq:pem}
\end{equation}
restricted to the confidence shell through $m_i$, with $\epsilon$ guarding the empty-mask case. No labels or pseudo-labels are used, and fine-tuning runs for a small fixed $E$ at a small learning rate.

\section{Analysis: Why PEM Works}
\label{sec:analysis}
\textit{The gradient.} The analysis is stated for a binary voxel with logit $z$ and probability $p=\sigma(z)$; the multi-class case replaces $z\,p(1-p)$ with the analogous per-logit softmax term. The per-voxel entropy gradient is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial H(p(z))}{\partial z} \;=\; \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\cdot\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} \;=\; (-z)\cdot p(1-p) \;=\; -\,z\,p(1-p).
\label{eq:dHdz}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Boundary-band gradient support]
\label{prop:gate}
For the unmasked entropy loss, the magnitude of each voxel's gradient is proportional to $|z_i|\,p_i(1{-}p_i)$, which vanishes at $p_i\to0$, at $p_i\to1$, and at $p_i=0.5$, and is maximized at $|z_i|\approx1.54$. The gradient is thus automatically supported on the moderate-confidence band flanking the decision surface, with no explicit confidence mask.
\end{proposition}

\textit{Gating versus normalization.} Proposition~\ref{prop:gate} fixes which voxels receive a gradient but not the step size, and the two reconcile Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. Gating is automatic and the mask leaves it unchanged; what the mask changes is the normalization, since Eq.~\ref{eq:pem} is a masked mean over $\sum_i m_i$ whereas unmasked it averages over a volume that is $91$ to $95\%$ saturated, diluting the shell by $15$ to $20\times$. What this buys is asymmetric. On the instance-normalized Pancreas checkpoint, which is robust, the unmasked loss is in fact better, by $0.12$ and $0.08$ Dice at the two learning rates; on the batch-normalized LA checkpoints, which are brittle, dilution is the difference between a gain and a collapse, costing up to $11$ Dice points unmasked and $30$ at the loosest threshold. The mask is therefore insurance rather than a source of gain.

\textit{Two bounded properties.} \emph{(i)} At small learning rate and high $\rho_f$ sharpening does not collapse a converged checkpoint, the gradient vanishing on committed voxels; the bound weakens as $\rho_f$ falls or the rate grows, where PEM degrades the checkpoint. \emph{(ii)} The mean gradient scales with $\bar V=\mathbb{E}_i[p_i(1{-}p_i)]$, so as $\rho_f\to1$ and $\bar V\to0$ the gain diminishes and pseudo-label fine-tuning, whose gradient does not vanish on committed voxels, becomes competitive.

\textit{Refinement, not relocation.} The step has the sign of $z_i$, committing a boundary voxel to its more probable class, so it refines a boundary that SSL convergence placed correctly and reinforces the error otherwise. This is why the effect is largest on the surface metrics and why $\rho_f$, which measures whether the uncertain voxels form a shell or scatter through the interior, is the quantity that indicates in advance which of the two to expect~\cite{chapelle2006ssl,grandvalet2004entropy}.

\section{Datasets and Implementation}
\label{sec:data}
\noindent\textbf{Datasets.}
Pancreas-CT~\cite{roth2015pancreas} (abdominal CT) is split into 12 labeled, 50 unlabeled and 18 test cases (20\% labeled); Left Atrium (LA) 2018 (cardiac MRI) at 5\% (4 labeled) and 10\% (8 labeled) with 20 test cases. Both use the preprocessing distributed with BCP~\cite{bai2023bcp}.

\noindent\textbf{Checkpoints and protocol.}
Experiments start from the publicly released BCP checkpoints (V-Net; instance normalization for Pancreas, batch normalization for LA). PEM uses Adam with gradient clipping at $1$, no weight decay or schedule, and LA batch-normalization statistics frozen. The budgets $E=2$ (Pancreas) and $E=5$ (LA) are retained from the submitted version, where they were set with test-set learning curves in view; they are not re-selected, and the final epoch is reported. All numbers are means over seeds $\{2020,42,123\}$. Inference uses sliding windows with BCP's strides and no test-time augmentation; each run takes under $5.2$ minutes on one RTX 4070 Ti SUPER.

\noindent\textbf{Selection protocol.}
No post-hoc method here optimizes on labels, so the labeled training volumes ($12$ Pancreas, $4$ and $8$ LA) are never seen by any of them and serve as the set for choosing the learning rate and mask threshold, held out from the post-hoc step though not from base training. This is not cross-validation: no fold is trained on, because no fold is ever used. Selection maximizes the Dice \emph{change} these volumes report, never their absolute Dice, which is optimistic because the base checkpoint was fitted on them. Checked retrospectively over $33$ configurations, the $30$ of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and three learning-rate-ceiling runs, that change ranks configurations as the test change does, with Spearman $\rho$ of $0.78$ (Pancreas), $0.75$ (LA 5\%) and $0.99$ (LA 10\%), and $0.67$ to $0.96$ once outright collapses are removed, so it is not merely separating collapse from non-collapse. The rule selects $\tau_m=0.90$ in all three settings, at $5\times10^{-5}$ (Pancreas) and $5\times10^{-6}$ (LA), and every baseline is put through it over the identical grid.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics and post-hoc baselines.}
Metrics are Dice and Jaccard (higher better) and HD95 and Average Surface Distance (ASD) in voxels (lower better). Three post-hoc baselines receive the same checkpoint, budget, data and selection rule: \textit{Temperature Scaling} (TS) fits a scalar temperature and cannot change the argmax; \textit{Pseudo-Label Fine-Tuning} (PL-FT) generates hard pseudo-labels (argmax then largest connected component) and fine-tunes with cross-entropy plus Dice; \textit{Self-Consistency} (SC) minimizes the squared error between the student on a noised input and the frozen teacher on the clean one. The comparison is against BCP because MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} release training code only.

\noindent\textbf{Residual entropy is concentrated (Table~\ref{tab:entropy}).}
The three released BCP checkpoints carry $89$--$95\%$ of their prediction entropy in a shell of only $5$--$9\%$ of the unlabeled voxels, giving $\rho_f(0.99)\in\{0.894,0.948,0.945\}$, all clearing $\rho^\star=0.85$. \noindent\textbf{PEM refines boundaries (Table~\ref{tab:main}).}
TS moves Dice by at most $0.03$, so its row is omitted: none of this is recalibration, a monotone rescaling being unable to move an argmax. PEM improves every metric on all three checkpoints, by $+1.02$, $+0.66$ and $+0.62$ Dice and by $24.8\%$, $44.0\%$ and $30.5\%$ of HD95, stably across seeds ($\pm0.05$, $\pm0.15$, $\pm0.06$). In mean HD95 it is also the best post-hoc method on Pancreas and LA 5\% ($5.87$ against $7.62$, and $7.79$ against $10.11$), but Table~\ref{tab:sig} shows only the Pancreas advantage to be reliable. The two metrics move on different timescales: on LA 5\% HD95 falls from $13.91$ to $9.18$ voxels in one epoch, two thirds of its total gain, while Dice dips to $87.11$ before recovering to $88.03$ by epoch~$5$. Both curves are flat by the end of the budget, and the reported epoch is the last rather than the best.

\noindent\textbf{What is, and is not, significant (Table~\ref{tab:sig}).}
Each case's metric is averaged over the three seeds before testing, so seed-case observations are never pooled; tests are two-sided paired Wilcoxon, HD95 the primary family and Dice secondary, with Holm correction across the three settings within each. On Pancreas the boundary gain is unambiguous: PEM lowers HD95 against BCP on $17$ of $18$ cases and beats PL-FT and SC after correction ($p_H\le.003$), while Dice improves more weakly ($p_H=.042$), the ordering a boundary-refinement step should produce. On LA the HD95 gains over BCP are nominal only ($p_H=.059$), Dice is significant on LA 10\% alone, and PEM is indistinguishable from PL-FT on both metrics; its mean HD95 lead on LA 5\% rests on a few cases, PEM being better on only $9$ of $20$. PL-FT does beat SC on both LA settings ($p_H\le.006$). PEM's Dice margin over PL-FT tracks headroom, $+0.73$, $-0.26$, $-0.07$ at base Dice $82.89$, $87.32$, $89.40$, which is consequence (ii) of Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}: the entropy gradient vanishes as predictions commit, the pseudo-label gradient does not.

\noindent\textbf{Mask and threshold ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).}
A tight $\tau_m=0.90$ isolates the boundary band while a loose $\tau_m=0.999$ readmits the interior and approaches the unmasked loss; the count of degraded cells rises monotonically along that path, from $0$ to $3$, and failures reach $30$ Dice points. $\tau_m=0.95$ attains the largest single gain, but its advantage over $\tau_m=0.99$ is unstable at a fixed budget, three cells won and three lost, by as little as $0.03$. $\tau_m=0.90$ is recommended: it alone never degrades a BCP checkpoint and has the highest mean over the six cells ($+0.72$ against $+0.46$). Below $10^{-6}$ no update occurs, and at $5\times10^{-5}$ the batch-normalized LA checkpoints lose $80$ and $85$ Dice while instance-normalized Pancreas is stable. On Pancreas a disagreement mask~\cite{yu2019uamt} is empty on a converged checkpoint, and decoder-only or EMA-only updates give $\le0$ gain, so PEM updates all student parameters, unlike normalization-only TTA~\cite{wang2021tent}.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Converged SSL checkpoints leave a structured, boundary-localized residual entropy; $\rho_f$ quantifies it without labels and PEM exploits it. On the three BCP checkpoints, which clear the threshold, minutes of label-free fine-tuning reduce mean HD95 by $25$ to $44\%$ and raise Dice by $+0.6$ to $+1.0$, the HD95 gain being significant on Pancreas. Both failure modes were visible from one forward pass: diffuse entropy ($\rho_f=0.55$, where the selected configuration loses $58$ Dice) and vanishing entropy ($\rho_f\to1$).

\noindent\textbf{Practical guidance.} Compute $\rho_f(0.99)$ from one label-free forward pass; if it clears $\rho^\star$ and the base Dice leaves headroom, fine-tune at $\tau_m=0.90$, the only threshold that degraded no SSL checkpoint here, with the learning rate set by normalization type: $5\times10^{-6}$ for batch-normalized checkpoints, destroyed at $5\times10^{-5}$, and up to $5\times10^{-5}$ for instance-normalized ones.

Three limitations bound these claims: the budgets $E$ were retained from the original submission rather than re-selected; $\rho^\star$ rests on four checkpoints and so is coarse; and $18$ to $20$ test volumes separate PEM from pseudo-label fine-tuning only in Pancreas HD95, not on Left Atrium. A denser sweep would address all three.
