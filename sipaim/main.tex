\begin{abstract}
Semi-supervised learning methods for medical image segmentation, or SSL methods, stop using the unlabeled data once training converges, even though the model's predictions on it still carry residual uncertainty concentrated at organ boundaries. This paper shows that this residual entropy is spatially structured and exploits it with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}, or PEM: any converged SSL checkpoint is fine-tuned for two to five epochs with a single entropy-minimization loss on unlabeled data, using no labels, no pseudo-labels, and no architectural changes. A label-free diagnostic $\rho_f$ predicts in advance when PEM will help, and a gradient analysis explains why the loss localizes to the boundary with no explicit mask, and why masking nonetheless helps, through the loss normalization rather than the gating. Every hyperparameter is chosen on the labeled training volumes, which no post-hoc objective ever sees, so no number is tuned on the test split. Applied to the publicly released Bidirectional Copy-Paste, or BCP, checkpoints, PEM reduces the 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance by $25$ to $44\%$ and improves the Dice score by $+1.02$, $+0.67$, and $+0.62$ points on Pancreas-CT at 20\% labeled, Left Atrium at 5\% labeled, and Left Atrium at 10\% labeled, in minutes per run on a single GPU. Against temperature scaling, pseudo-label fine-tuning, and self-consistency under an identical protocol and selection rule, the comparison separates by metric rather than by dataset: PEM is the best method on both boundary metrics wherever the checkpoint retains headroom, by a $23\%$ margin in HD95 over the nearest competitor, and on all four metrics on Pancreas-CT, while on Left Atrium the three methods are statistically indistinguishable in Dice. A mask ablation read at a fixed budget identifies one confidence threshold that degrades none of the checkpoints tested, where looser thresholds cost up to $30$ Dice points.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Manual voxel-level annotation of 3D medical scans is slow and expensive, which makes semi-supervised learning, or SSL, training from a small labeled set together with a large unlabeled set, a practical necessity for volumetric segmentation. Recent SSL methods for this task~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher,yu2019uamt,bai2023bcp,liu2024most,assefa2025dycon} reach strong accuracy by using the unlabeled data during training and then discarding it at convergence.

This paper makes a complementary observation: at convergence, a well-trained SSL model still assigns non-trivial prediction entropy to the unlabeled volumes, concentrated in a thin shell of low-confidence voxels on the predicted organ boundary. This concentration is a property of SSL convergence, not of any single method, and it is exactly the condition under which a label-free sharpening step is well-behaved, since the gradient it produces is already aligned with the locus of segmentation error.

This is exploited with \textit{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}, or PEM: any converged SSL checkpoint is fine-tuned for a few epochs on the unlabeled data with a single entropy objective, using no labels, pseudo-labels, thresholds, or architectural changes, in minutes on one GPU, on top of a released checkpoint without retraining. Entropy minimization itself is classical~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} and standard in modern SSL~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch}; what is new is its operating regime and the three results that follow.

\noindent\textbf{A label-free diagnostic.}
The first contribution is the \textit{shell entropy concentration} $\rho_f$, a scalar that turns the observation above into a test. For a converged checkpoint, $\rho_f$ is the fraction of total prediction entropy that the low-confidence voxels carry on the unlabeled set; it needs neither labels nor a validation set and follows from a single forward pass. It separates checkpoints on which post-hoc sharpening is well-conditioned from those on which it is not: the three released BCP checkpoints hold $89$ to $95\%$ of their prediction entropy in a thin boundary shell of a few percent of the voxels, whereas a supervised-only network on the same labels keeps only $55\%$ there and scatters the rest through the interior, as Table~\ref{tab:entropy} reports.

\noindent\textbf{PEM and why it works.}
The second contribution is PEM together with an account of why the classical loss needs no stabilizers in this regime. The entropy-minimization gradient is supported on a band of moderate confidence flanking the boundary and vanishes both on the saturated interior and at the point of maximum ambiguity, so it gates itself to the boundary shell with no explicit mask; the confidence mask instead acts through the loss normalization. Because partial-volume averaging and weak soft-tissue contrast make the anatomical-boundary voxels the ambiguous ones, the feature-space decision surface co-locates with the physical organ surface, and entropy minimization there realizes the cluster assumption as boundary refinement. The same argument predicts the two regimes in which PEM should fail, and the entropy maps in Fig.~\ref{fig:qual} show the boundary shell it acts on.

\noindent\textbf{Validation across organs and modalities.}
The third contribution is an empirical study spanning two organs and two imaging modalities, run under a selection rule that keeps the test split out of every decision. Because no post-hoc method here optimizes on labels, the labeled training volumes are a legitimate held-out set for choosing learning rates and thresholds, and the same rule is applied to PEM and to each baseline. Under it PEM improves every metric on all three released BCP checkpoints and is the best method on both boundary metrics wherever the checkpoint retains headroom, as Table~\ref{tab:main} summarizes, although on Left Atrium the three post-hoc methods are not statistically separable in Dice. The ablation of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, read at the fixed budget, isolates the confidence-shell design and identifies the one threshold that degrades none of the checkpoints tested; and PEM degrades a checkpoint only when its $\rho_f$ falls below the suitability threshold, exactly as the diagnostic predicts.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\noindent\textbf{SSL for medical image segmentation.}
Two mechanisms dominate: \emph{consistency regularization}, where a student network is trained to agree with a teacher or with itself under perturbations, and \emph{pseudo-labeling}, where confident predictions become hard targets. The Mean Teacher framework~\cite{tarvainen2017meanteacher} uses an exponential-moving-average (EMA) teacher; Uncertainty-Aware Mean Teacher (UA-MT)~\cite{yu2019uamt} filters the target by uncertainty; DTC~\cite{luo2022dtc} and MC-Net+~\cite{wu2022mcnet} enforce consistency across heads or decoders; Cross Pseudo Supervision (CPS)~\cite{chen2021cps} combines both. Bidirectional Copy-Paste (BCP)~\cite{bai2023bcp} mixes labeled and unlabeled crops bidirectionally and is a strong, widely used baseline; MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} improve further but release training code only. All share a training-time contract: the unlabeled data is used during optimization and never revisited. PEM revisits what it still contains at convergence; the novelty is not entropy minimization but characterizing the SSL-convergence regime, with a label-free diagnostic, in which it becomes well-conditioned.

\noindent\textbf{Entropy minimization.}
Entropy minimization~\cite{grandvalet2004entropy} adds $-\lambda H(Y\mid X)$ on unlabeled data and implements the low-density-separation principle of the cluster assumption~\cite{chapelle2006ssl}; Pseudo-Label~\cite{lee2013pseudolabel} is its hard-target limit, and modern methods such as FixMatch~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch} sharpen predictions to form pseudo-targets. In all of these the entropy term is active \emph{during} training, coupled to a supervised or consistency loss that prevents posterior collapse, and requires careful balancing over time. PEM uses the same formal loss but in a different regime: applied to an already-converged checkpoint, the residual entropy it sees is concentrated at boundaries, so the gradient is self-localized and none of the usual stabilizing machinery is needed.

\noindent\textbf{Test-time adaptation.}
The closest methodology is test-time adaptation. TENT~\cite{wang2021tent} minimizes prediction entropy on a test stream by updating only normalization parameters; SHOT~\cite{liang2020shot} adapts a frozen classifier by entropy minimization with information maximization; and EATA~\cite{niu2022eata} and SAR~\cite{niu2023sar} add entropy-based sample selection, the same masking question as Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, and document the collapse unrestricted entropy minimization can cause. All share PEM's loss but assume a distribution shift, adapting a parameter subset against a moving target stream. PEM makes no shift assumption, operates on training-distribution data, updates all parameters, and yields a single frozen-weights checkpoint; in the SSL-convergence regime the collapse they guard against is bounded away (Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}).

\noindent\textbf{Post-hoc calibration and refinement.}
Temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration} rescales logits by a scalar and, being monotone, cannot change the argmax and hence cannot move any overlap metric; it is included to rule out recalibration. Stochastic weight averaging~\cite{izmailov2018swa} and self-distillation~\cite{hinton2015distillation} refine a model without new labels but need the training trajectory or hard-target distillation, closer to the pseudo-label baseline.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Shell Entropy Concentration}
Let $f_{\theta^*}$ be a converged checkpoint and $\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}$ an unlabeled volume. For voxel $i$ with per-class softmax probabilities $p_{i,c}$, define the per-voxel entropy $H_i=-\sum_c p_{i,c}\log p_{i,c}$ and confidence $c_i=\max_c p_{i,c}$. The \textit{shell entropy concentration} at confidence threshold $\tau$ is
\begin{equation}
\rho_f(\tau) \;=\; \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\,\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau]\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{U}}\!\left[\sum_{i} H_i\right]} \in [0,1].
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
$\rho_f(\tau)$ is the fraction of total prediction entropy carried by voxels of confidence at most $\tau$. It is label-free, scalar, and self-localizing: on a converged network the low-confidence voxels lie near the decision boundary. The diagnostic is always evaluated at $\tau=0.99$, and $\rho^\star=0.85$ is a provisional suitability threshold, placed below the SSL checkpoints and well above the supervised baseline of Table~\ref{tab:entropy} and left to be pinned down by the denser checkpoint sweep discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion}. The loss of Sec.~\ref{sec:method}-B uses a separate threshold $\tau_m$, written distinctly because the two serve different purposes and take different values: $\tau$ measures where the entropy sits, $\tau_m$ decides which voxels the loss averages over.

\subsection{Post-hoc Entropy Minimization}
PEM fine-tunes the checkpoint on the unlabeled set with the entropy loss
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{PEM}} \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{U}}\frac{\sum_{i} m_i H_i}{\sum_i m_i+\epsilon},
\qquad m_i=\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau_m],
\label{eq:pem}
\end{equation}
optionally restricted to the confidence shell through the mask $m_i$, with $\epsilon$ a small constant guarding the empty-mask case. No labels or pseudo-labels are used. Fine-tuning runs for a small, fixed number of epochs $E$ with a small learning rate.

\section{Analysis: Why PEM Works}
\label{sec:analysis}
\textit{The gradient.} The analysis is stated for a binary voxel with logit $z$ and probability $p=\sigma(z)$; the multi-class case replaces $z\,p(1-p)$ with the analogous per-logit softmax term. The per-voxel entropy gradient is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial H(p(z))}{\partial z} \;=\; \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\cdot\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} \;=\; (-z)\cdot p(1-p) \;=\; -\,z\,p(1-p).
\label{eq:dHdz}
\end{equation}
This product vanishes at three points, $p\to0$, $p\to1$, and $p=0.5$ where $z=0$, and is maximized on the moderately confident voxels at $|z|\approx1.54$, i.e.\ $p\approx0.82$ or $0.18$. The entropy gradient is therefore supported on a band of moderate confidence flanking the boundary: negligible on the saturated interior and at maximum ambiguity, largest on the partially decided voxels beside it.

\begin{proposition}[Boundary-band gradient support]
\label{prop:gate}
For the unmasked entropy loss, the magnitude of each voxel's gradient is proportional to $|z_i|\,p_i(1{-}p_i)$, which vanishes at $p_i\to0$, at $p_i\to1$, and at $p_i=0.5$, and is maximized at $|z_i|\approx1.54$. The gradient is thus automatically supported on the moderate-confidence band flanking the decision surface, with no explicit confidence mask.
\end{proposition}

\textit{Gating versus normalization.} Proposition~\ref{prop:gate} fixes which voxels receive a gradient but not the step size, and the two reconcile Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. The gating is automatic and the mask leaves it unchanged; the mask changes the normalization. Eq.~\ref{eq:pem} is a masked mean over $\sum_i m_i$; unmasked it averages over the whole volume, $91$ to $95\%$ of which is saturated, diluting the shell by $15$ to $20\times$. Masking removes the dilution and scales the effective shell step, although both losses gate the same voxels; a looser threshold readmits the interior and recovers the weaker behavior. What this buys is asymmetric, and Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows the shape of it. Where the checkpoint is robust, under instance normalization on Pancreas, the unmasked loss is no worse, in fact better by $0.08$ Dice at both learning rates. Where it is brittle, under batch normalization on LA, dilution is the difference between a gain and a collapse, costing up to $11$ Dice points unmasked and $30$ at the loosest threshold. The mask is therefore best read as insurance rather than as a source of gain.

\textit{Two bounded properties.} \emph{(i)} At small learning rate and high $\rho_f$, sharpening does not collapse a converged checkpoint, since the gradient vanishes on committed voxels; the bound weakens as $\rho_f$ falls or the learning rate grows, where PEM degrades the checkpoint, as on the supervised baseline and above $5\times10^{-5}$. \emph{(ii)} The mean gradient scales with $\bar V=\mathbb{E}_i[p_i(1{-}p_i)]$, so as $\rho_f\to1$ and $\bar V\to0$ the gain diminishes and pseudo-label fine-tuning, whose gradient does not vanish on committed voxels, becomes competitive.

\textit{Refinement, not relocation.} The step has the sign of $z_i$, committing a boundary voxel to its more probable class, so it refines a boundary that SSL convergence placed correctly and reinforces the error otherwise. Because partial-volume averaging and weak soft-tissue contrast make the anatomical-boundary voxels the ambiguous ones, the feature-space decision surface co-locates with the physical organ surface, so entropy minimization realizes the cluster assumption~\cite{chapelle2006ssl,grandvalet2004entropy} as boundary refinement; $\rho_f$ measures whether the uncertain voxels form that shell or scatter through the interior.

\section{Datasets and Implementation}
\label{sec:data}
\noindent\textbf{Datasets.}
Evaluation uses two public benchmarks. Pancreas-CT~\cite{roth2015pancreas} contains abdominal Computed Tomography (CT) scans, split into 12 labeled, 50 unlabeled, and 18 test cases (20\% labeled). The Left Atrium (LA) 2018 dataset contains cardiac Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) scans, split at 5\% (4 labeled) and 10\% (8 labeled) with 20 test cases. Both use the preprocessing distributed with BCP~\cite{bai2023bcp}.

\noindent\textbf{Checkpoints and protocol.}
The experiments start from the publicly released BCP checkpoints (V-Net backbone; instance normalization for Pancreas, batch normalization for LA). PEM uses Adam (no weight decay or schedule) and gradient clipping at $1$; LA batch-normalization statistics are frozen. The budget is fixed \textit{a priori} at $E=2$ (Pancreas) and $E=5$ (LA); the epoch is not selected, the final one is reported. All numbers are the mean over seeds $\{2020,42,123\}$. Inference uses sliding windows with BCP's strides and no test-time augmentation (TTA); each run finishes in under $5.2$ minutes on one RTX 4070 Ti SUPER.

\noindent\textbf{Selection protocol.}
PEM optimizes an entropy objective on the unlabeled split alone, so the labeled training volumes, $12$ for Pancreas and $4$ and $8$ for LA, are never seen by any post-hoc objective and serve as a held-out set for choosing the learning rate and the mask threshold. This is not cross-validation: no fold is ever trained on, because no post-hoc method trains on labels at all. Selection maximizes the Dice \emph{change} these volumes report, never their absolute Dice, which is optimistic because the base SSL checkpoint was fitted on them. Across the $33$ configurations of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} that change ranks configurations as the test change does, with Spearman $\rho$ of $0.76$ (Pancreas), $0.76$ (LA 5\%) and $0.99$ (LA 10\%), and $0.67$ to $0.96$ when the configurations that collapse outright are removed, so the ranking is not merely separating collapse from non-collapse. The rule selects the confident-shell loss at $\tau_m=0.90$ in all three settings, with learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$ (Pancreas) and $5\times10^{-6}$ (LA). Every post-hoc baseline is put through the identical rule over the identical learning-rate grid, so no method sees the test split before it is reported.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics and post-hoc baselines.}
The metrics are Dice and Jaccard (higher is better) and the 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance (HD95) and Average Surface Distance (ASD) in voxels (lower is better). Three post-hoc baselines receive the same checkpoint, budget, learning rate, and data:
\textit{Temperature Scaling} (TS) fits a scalar temperature on unlabeled entropy and cannot change the argmax;
\textit{Pseudo-Label Fine-Tuning} (PL-FT) generates hard pseudo-labels (argmax then largest connected component, CC) and fine-tunes with cross-entropy plus Dice;
\textit{Self-Consistency} (SC) minimizes the mean-squared error between the student on a noised input and the frozen teacher on the clean input.
The comparison is against BCP because MOST~\cite{liu2024most} and DyCON~\cite{assefa2025dycon} release training code only.

\noindent\textbf{The diagnostic is predictive.}
Applying PEM to the supervised checkpoint ($\rho_f=0.552$, below $\rho^\star$) instead \emph{reduces} Dice from $75.66$ to $69.41$ ($-6.25$) within one epoch: with no boundary shell, sharpening reinforces errors, as the analysis predicts, so this label-free check can be run before committing.

\noindent\textbf{PEM refines boundaries (Table~\ref{tab:main}).}
TS moves Dice by at most $0.03$ anywhere, so its row is omitted: nothing reported here is recalibration, a monotone rescaling of the logits being unable to move an argmax. PEM improves every metric on all three checkpoints, by $+1.02$, $+0.67$ and $+0.62$ Dice and by $24.9\%$, $44.0\%$ and $30.5\%$ of HD95, and is stable across seeds ($\pm0.05$, $\pm0.15$, $\pm0.06$). Against the other post-hoc methods the comparison separates by metric rather than by setting. On the boundary metrics PEM is best wherever the checkpoint still has headroom, with HD95 of $5.87$ against $7.62$ for the nearest competitor on Pancreas and $7.79$ against $10.11$ on LA 5\%, a $23\%$ margin over the next best method in both cases, and ASD following. On Dice it leads only on Pancreas, whose base checkpoint is furthest from saturation at $82.89$. The split is what a boundary-refinement step should produce, since HD95 and ASD measure surface agreement, which is where the entropy gradient acts, whereas Dice is volumetric and dominated by the interior that PEM deliberately leaves untouched.

\noindent\textbf{What is, and is not, significant (Table~\ref{tab:sig}).}
Per-case paired tests qualify these gains and rule out an overclaim. Against BCP, PEM is significant on Pancreas and LA 10\% ($p=0.030$ for both) but \emph{not} on LA 5\% ($p=0.097$), where the per-case spread is widest. Head to head, PEM leads PL-FT by $+0.73$ Dice on Pancreas (bootstrap CI $[+0.10,+1.39]$, $p=0.081$) and is indistinguishable from it on both LA settings ($p=0.729$ and $p=0.674$). PL-FT's nominal Dice lead on LA is therefore not evidence that pseudo-labelling is the better method there; twenty cases do not separate the three. PL-FT is, however, the more \emph{consistent} method, beating BCP on $16$ to $18$ of $20$ cases where PEM wins $12$ to $14$, a difference of variance rather than of mean. Across the three settings PEM's Dice margin over PL-FT tracks the base checkpoint's headroom, $+0.73$ at $82.89$, $-0.25$ at $87.32$ and $-0.07$ at $89.40$, which is consequence (ii) of Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis}: the entropy gradient scales with $\bar V$ and vanishes as predictions commit, while the pseudo-label gradient does not.

\noindent\textbf{Mask and threshold ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).}
Read at the fixed budget, the ablation says something sharper than a ranking of thresholds: it says how much of the mask's value is insurance. Since $m_i=\mathbb{1}[c_i\le\tau_m]$, a tight $\tau_m=0.90$ isolates the boundary band while a loose $\tau_m=0.999$ readmits the interior and approaches the unmasked loss, and the count of degraded cells rises monotonically along exactly that path, from $0$ at $\tau_m=0.90$ to $3$ unmasked. The failures are not small: at $\tau_m=0.999$ the unmasked interior costs $30$ Dice points on LA 10\% at $10^{-5}$. This is the normalization argument of Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis} made visible, since the mask leaves the gating untouched and changes only what the loss averages over. The best \emph{mean} gain belongs to $\tau_m=0.95$, but its advantage does not survive the budget being fixed: $\tau_m=0.95$ beats $\tau_m=0.99$ in three of six cells and loses in three, with margins of $0.03$ to $0.06$ on Pancreas, so neither threshold is recommended and $\tau_m=0.90$ is, on the ground that it is the only one that never degrades a checkpoint. The learning rate has a hard floor and ceiling: below $10^{-6}$ no update occurs, and at $5\times10^{-5}$ the batch-normalized LA checkpoints are destroyed outright, losing $80$ and $85$ Dice points, while Pancreas under instance normalization is stable there. Two further choices are confirmed on Pancreas-CT: a disagreement mask~\cite{yu2019uamt} is empty on a converged checkpoint and yields no update, so the confidence shell is the right post-hoc mask; and updating only the decoder or EMA weights gives $\le0$ gain, so PEM updates all student parameters, unlike normalization-only TTA~\cite{wang2021tent}.

\noindent\textbf{The boundary moves first.}
The two metrics respond on different timescales, and the order is the one the mechanism implies. On LA 5\% the HD95 falls from $13.91$ to $9.18$ voxels in a single epoch, two thirds of its total improvement, while Dice in that same epoch dips slightly below baseline, to $87.11$, before recovering and rising to $88.03$ by epoch~$5$. Sharpening the boundary shell therefore buys surface accuracy immediately and overlap only as the refinement accumulates, which is consistent with a step that moves boundary voxels rather than relabelling regions. Both curves are flat by the end of the budget, so $E\in\{2,5\}$ is not a lucky early stop; and since the reported epoch is the last rather than the best, no cherry-picking is possible.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This work shows that converged SSL checkpoints leave a structured, boundary-localized residual entropy, quantifies it with the label-free diagnostic $\rho_f$, and exploits it with Post-hoc Entropy Minimization. Whenever $\rho_f(0.99)$ clears the threshold, as for all three BCP checkpoints but not the supervised baseline, a few minutes of label-free fine-tuning reduces HD95 by $25$ to $44\%$ and improves Dice by $+0.6$ to $+1.0$ points, with the boundary metrics moving first and furthest.

PEM has two predictable failure modes, both diagnosable from one forward pass: diffuse entropy ($\rho_f\lesssim0.6$, the supervised checkpoint, where PEM loses $6.25$ Dice) and vanishing entropy ($\rho_f\to1$, LA 10\%, where the gradient shrinks and pseudo-label fine-tuning catches up).

\noindent\textbf{Practical guidance.} PEM is a decision rule, not a universal step. Given a released checkpoint, $\rho_f(0.99)$ follows from one label-free forward pass; if it clears $\rho^\star$ and the base Dice leaves headroom, the checkpoint is fine-tuned with the confident-shell loss at $\tau_m=0.90$, the only threshold that degraded no checkpoint tested here. The learning rate should be set by normalization type rather than copied across datasets: $5\times10^{-6}$ for batch-normalized checkpoints, which are destroyed outright at $5\times10^{-5}$, and up to $5\times10^{-5}$ for instance-normalized ones, which are stable there. Expect the largest effect on surface metrics, and expect overlap gains to shrink toward those of pseudo-labelling as the checkpoint saturates.

Two limitations bound these claims. First, the diagnostic rests on four checkpoints, so $\rho^\star$ and the $\rho_f$-to-gain relationship are coarse and the single supervised negative is confounded. Second, with $18$ to $20$ test volumes the per-case tests separate PEM from pseudo-label fine-tuning on Pancreas only, and there only at $p=0.08$; the Dice ordering on Left Atrium is not established in either direction, and a larger test set would be needed to settle it. The next experiment addresses both: a BCP checkpoint sweep over training lengths and independent seeds, plus public SSL methods (UA-MT~\cite{yu2019uamt}, DTC~\cite{luo2022dtc}, MC-Net+~\cite{wu2022mcnet}, CPS~\cite{chen2021cps}) across label fractions, gives a family over which $\Delta$Dice regresses on $\rho_f$ and over which the post-hoc methods can be compared with power.
