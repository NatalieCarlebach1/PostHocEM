Speaker notes
0:20–1:20Semi-supervised methods such as BCP use a large unlabeled set during training, and then stop using it once training converges. We looked at what a converged model still "doesn't know" about that unlabeled data. On three BCP checkpoints (pancreas CT at 20% labels, and left atrium MRI at 5% and 10%), 89 to 95 percent of the prediction entropy sits in only 5 to 9 percent of the voxels. And those voxels are not random: they form a thin shell on the predicted organ surface, which is exactly where segmentation errors live. The interior is saturated. So the question of this paper is: can we use that leftover, spatially structured uncertainty after training, without any labels?

Shaharabany, Carlebach, Wolf · SIPAIM 20263
Speaker notes
1:20–2:20The method, Post-hoc Entropy Minimization or PEM, is deliberately simple. One: take any converged SSL checkpoint. In our experiments that is BCP: the released checkpoints for left atrium, and our reproduction of BCP for pancreas. Two: fine-tune it on the unlabeled volumes with a single loss, the mean entropy of the voxels whose confidence is below a threshold tau-m. We use tau-m = 0.90. There are no labels, no pseudo-labels, no teacher, no architectural change. Three: stop after a fixed budget, two epochs for pancreas and five for left atrium, and report the final epoch. The fine-tuning itself takes under a minute on one GPU. Entropy minimization is classical. What is new is the regime: applying it after convergence, when the remaining entropy already sits on the boundary.

The entropy gradient targets the boundary

∂H∂z=−zp(1−p)
  • Vanishes on the saturated interior (p → 0 or 1)
  • Vanishes at maximum ambiguity (p = 0.5)
  • Peaks on partly decided voxels (p ≈ 0.82), the band beside the boundary

So the unmasked loss already focuses on the boundary band. The mask adds insurance: it drops voxels above τm and renormalizes the loss, and our ablation does not separate those two effects.

Shaharabany, Carlebach, Wolf · SIPAIM 20264
Speaker notes
2:20–3:30Why does this work without any stabilizer? Look at the gradient of a voxel's entropy with respect to its logit: minus z times p times one-minus-p. It vanishes in three places: on the saturated interior, where p is near 0 or 1, and at maximum ambiguity, p = 0.5. It peaks at p of about 0.82. So the gradient is automatically supported on a band of partly decided voxels next to the decision surface, the boundary shell from the previous slide, with no mask at all. What does the mask add, then? At tau-m = 0.90 the gradient is still 88 percent of its peak, so the mask both removes those voxels and renormalizes the loss over the ones it keeps. Our ablation does not separate these two effects, so we describe the mask as insurance against collapse. We'll see the evidence for that in a moment.

Speaker notes
3:30–4:40Because the method hurts when the uncertainty is not boundary-shaped, we want a check we can run before fine-tuning, without labels. That is rho-f: the fraction of total prediction entropy held by the low-confidence voxels, from a single forward pass on the unlabeled data. The three BCP checkpoints all score about 0.9 or higher. A supervised-only network trained on the same 12 pancreas labels scores 0.55; its uncertainty is scattered through the interior. And on that checkpoint PEM fails badly: the very setting that improves BCP pancreas by about one Dice point collapses the supervised model from 75.7 to 17.3 Dice. Two caveats: the threshold of 0.85 rests on these four checkpoints only, and rho-f itself has no spatial term. That the uncertain voxels sit on the boundary we show separately, with their distance to the boundary.

Speaker notes
6:40–7:50Are these gains reliable? We ran per-case paired Wilcoxon tests, averaging each case over the three seeds first, with HD95 as the primary endpoint and Holm correction across the three settings. On pancreas the boundary gain is clear: PEM lowers HD95 against BCP on 17 of 18 cases, and beats both pseudo-labeling and self-consistency after correction. On left atrium it is not: the HD95 gains over BCP are nominal, raw p of .03 but .059 after correction, and we detect no significant difference from pseudo-label fine-tuning on either metric. We call these tests exploratory, and I want to be explicit why: the epoch budgets, the learning-rate grid bounds and the pancreas base checkpoint were informed by earlier test-set results, and a multiplicity correction does not remove that kind of selection bias. Our reading: PEM's advantage over pseudo-labeling shrinks as the checkpoint saturates, which is what the gradient analysis predicts, since the entropy gradient vanishes as predictions commit.

Speaker notes
7:50–8:50What does the mask buy? This is the ablation, read at each dataset's fixed endpoint, not at a peak. Loosening the mask from 0.90 toward 0.999 readmits the saturated interior and approaches the unmasked loss, and the number of degraded cells, in red, rises along that path from zero to three. The failures are large: the loosest mask costs 30 Dice points on LA 10%. tau-m = 0.90 is the only threshold that never degrades a BCP checkpoint, and it also has the highest mean gain. tau-m = 0.95 has the single largest cell, but it beats 0.99 in only three of six cells, so we don't recommend it. On the robust instance-normalized pancreas checkpoint the unmasked loss is slightly better. So the mask is insurance against collapse on brittle checkpoints, not a source of gain.

Takeaways

1

Residual entropy is structured

On converged BCP checkpoints it sits in a few low-confidence voxels on the organ surface. ρf measures the concentration from one forward pass.

2

A cheap post-hoc step

2–5 epochs, no labels, under a minute of fine-tuning: HD95 −25% to −44%, Dice +0.6 to +1.0. Clearest gain on Pancreas-CT.

3

Use it with care

Check ρf first, keep τm = 0.90, and set the learning rate by normalization type: 5×10−6 batch-norm, up to 5×10−5 instance-norm.

Limitations: the significance tests are exploratory; ρ* rests on four checkpoints; 18–20 test volumes.

How the settings were chosen

Selection rule

  • Labeled training volumes (12 Pancreas; 4 and 8 LA) are excluded from every post-hoc objective and used only to choose the learning rate and τm.
  • The rule maximizes their Dice change, not absolute Dice, since they also trained the base checkpoint.
  • The same rule and grid apply to PL-FT and SC; every method reports 3-seed means.
  • Five-fold cross-validation is impossible with four volumes, so this is a non-independent selection set; selection overfitting remains possible.

What is retrospective

  • This is a corrected retrospective reanalysis of the submitted study.
  • Budgets E = 2 (Pancreas) and E = 5 (LA) are carried over from the submitted protocol; grid bounds came from earlier sweeps.
  • Pancreas BCP is our reproduction (82.89 vs 82.91 reported), saved at its best test Dice, so its base number is optimistic.
  • Retrospective rank check over 33 configurations: validation vs test ΔDice, Spearman ρ = 0.78 / 0.75 / 0.99 (Pancreas / LA 5% / LA 10%).
